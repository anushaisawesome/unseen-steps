Audio description for studio dance shows

Experience the dance, not just the music.

Professional audio description of dance is rare and goes to large venues, so the shows put on by small studios, schools and competitions never have it. Unseen Steps gives those studios rich description of their own shows. Blind and partially sighted people in the audience hear what the dancers are doing, in the style's own terms, in the gaps in the music.

    How it works

    Before the show

    1. Upload the rehearsal

      The studio uploads a rehearsal video of the piece and its song.

    2. Argon studies it

      Gemini 4 Argon watches and listens to the whole piece, then looks closely at each section. It maps the style and its influences, what makes this choreography distinctive, the formations and canons, every change of texture, and the key moments with their timing.

    3. Find the gaps

      The song is mapped into beats, phrases and the quieter gaps where a line can be spoken without covering the music.

    4. Show notes

      Argon writes show notes in the style's own vocabulary, explained for someone who has never seen it. Every line is checked against the video at its moment. The notes are stored per show, and the studio can adjust them.

    During the show

    1. Track

      A camera follows the dancer and pose tracking measures the body many times a second: jumps, turns and their direction, facing, footwork, floor work and turnout.

    2. Narrate what happens

      Gemini Live holds the show notes and knows where it is in the song. In each gap it describes what the dancer is doing now, including anything different from rehearsal, starting to speak in under a second.

    3. Check

      Every claim is compared with the measured pose. If Gemini says "turns left" when nobody turned, the next line puts it right.

    4. Listen

      The narration goes to each listener's own phone or earphones, never over the room's music.

      Built to be trusted

      Checked, not guessed

      Every line in the show notes is checked against the video at its moment. Live, words like jump, turn, left, kick, floor or still must match the measured movement.

      The style's own terms, explained

      A term is used only when every part of it is visible (a plié needs turned-out knees), and it's explained the first time so a newcomer can picture it. The vocabulary, vetted by dancers, lives in MongoDB Atlas.

      Private by design

      Video frames are never stored, only body points. Narration goes to the listener's own earphones, never over the room's music.

      Studios stay in charge

      It covers the shows that would otherwise get no description. The studio, or a describer working with it, can correct the show notes, the style and the glossary.
