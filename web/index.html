Live audio description for dance

Every dance performance, described.

Audio description usually covers one or two shows in a run, and small shows get none. Unseen Steps describes the movement out loud for blind and partially sighted audiences, in the gaps in the music, and checks every line against the measured movement before it's spoken.

    How it works

    1. Watch

      A fixed camera follows the dancer and measures the body many times a second: where the limbs are, how high, which way she turns.

    2. Map the music

      The song is mapped in advance into beats, phrases and the quiet gaps where a line can be spoken without covering the music.

    3. Write

      Gemini watches the whole performance and writes a short, vivid line for each moment, timed to the music.

    4. Check

      Claims about movement are tested against the measurements. A line that says "turns left" when nobody turned is rewritten once, or dropped.

    5. Speak

      The line is voiced and played in the next gap in the music, a beat or two after the movement.

      Built to be trusted

      Checked, not guessed

      Words like jump, turn, left, kick, floor or still must match the measured movement. A line that fails is rewritten once, or dropped.

      Next: plain words first

      We're working on describing the shape before naming it ("feet crossed tightly, heel to toe: fifth position"), with a choice of plain, learning or fluent terminology, so newcomers can picture every move.

      Private by design

      Video frames are never stored, only body points. Narration goes to the listener's own earphones, never over the room's music.

      Describers stay in charge

      It covers the performances that would otherwise get no description. A human describer can correct the style, the dancer to follow and the glossary.
