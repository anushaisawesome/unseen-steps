Live venue demo

Listen to a dancer, live

At a studio's show, a camera faces the stage and each blind or partially sighted person in the audience hears the description on their own phone or earphones, in the quieter moments of the music. This demo runs both halves on one device: point its camera at one dancer, whole body in view, and press Start listening.

To write each line, two small still frames a second and the body measurements go to Gemini. Nothing is recorded or stored.

How much to describe

Director's notes

Every 20 seconds, Gemini Argon watches the last stretch and updates these notes on the style, the dancer's signature, recurring motifs and what just changed. The live describer uses them, so lines get more specific as the piece goes on. At a studio show, the notes Argon prepared from the rehearsal video are loaded here before the curtain goes up.

What it said

Newest first: what was measured, the line, whether it was spoken, and any line the checker rejected. To stay live, a line that's ready too late for the movement it describes is skipped, not spoken late.

    Getting in at a real show

    Scanning a QR code is a visual task, so a studio offers other ways to open the listening page too:

    • a short web address the announcer reads out before the show
    • a link in the ticket email
    • an NFC tag on the seat or the programme
    • front-of-house staff who can open it on your phone

    The description goes to your own earphones, while the music plays from the room's speakers.
